Methodology

This is a list with sources, not a soundtrack leak. Every row is labelled by how close it gets to a first-party, citeable source.

Artist-reported rows

Link the artist’s own post first

For artist-reported rows, try to link the artist’s own post (Instagram, X, official site, or an interview on their channel). Aggregators and news roundups are discovery only until that primary permalink is attached. Rows without it stay marked as needing a primary source.

The three tiers

Strongest evidence to weakest

01

Official promo

You can hear the track in a trailer or extended look posted by the publisher.

Rockstar YouTube is a first-party source. Polygon, Music Ally, GTA Wiki, and NME help name the song. Fan compilations are not enough on their own.

02

Rockstar-named

A Rockstar staff member names an artist in an interview, but not necessarily a song.

Stronger than a rumour. Weaker than hearing the record in a trailer.

03

Artist-reported

An artist or representative says they are involved, and we can cite that claim.

Link a primary post or interview they gave. Aggregators stay as discovery until that URL exists.

Off the list

What does not go in

  • Rumours presented as reporting
  • Aggregators used as proof
  • Anonymous “insider” screenshots
  • A soundtrack credit without a link

Hosting

Link out. Do not host files.

Cite official media and interviews. Link out. Do not host Rockstar media, lyrics, or copied bios.

  1. Claim
  2. Find primary link
  3. Check context
  4. Label tier
  5. Publish with sources
