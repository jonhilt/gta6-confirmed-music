How the tracker decides what counts

Show us the receipts.

This is documentation, not prediction. Every row is labelled by how close it gets to a first-party, citeable source.

Plain speech / zero rumour mills

The three tiers

Strongest evidence ↔ weakest evidence

01

Official promo

You can hear the track in a trailer or extended look posted by the publisher.

Rockstar YouTube is a first-party source. Polygon, Music Ally, GTA Wiki, and NME help name the song. Fan compilations are not enough on their own.

02

Rockstar-named

A Rockstar staff member names an artist in an interview, but not necessarily a song.

Stronger than a rumour. Weaker than hearing the record in a trailer.

03

Artist-reported

An artist or representative says they are involved, and we can tie that claim.

Link a primary post or interview they gave. Aggregators stay as discovery until that URL exists.

The hard no list

What we refuse

  • Rumours dressed up as reporting
  • Aggregators used as proof
  • Anonymous insider screenshots
  • A soundtrack credit without a link

Editorial rules

Link out, don't lift.

Cite official media and interviews. Link out. Do not host Rockstar media, lyrics, or copied bios.

  1. Claim
  2. Find primary link
  3. Check context
  4. Label tier
  5. Publish with sources
